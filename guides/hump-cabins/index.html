What is a hump cabin on a cruise ship?

By the Which Cabin editorial team. How we check a page.

Short answer: a hump cabin is the passenger name for a stateroom on the part of the hull that steps outward, usually around midship. Cruise Critic's cabin guide, checked 20 Nov 2026, calls that curve the hump and says cabins where the hull goes from narrower to wider can have angled balconies that are a bit roomier than a typical balcony. It does not say the inside of the room is larger. We do not name a ship as having one unless that ship's own deck plan shows the step.

Why some of them are bigger

The extra width of the hull has to land somewhere. On the ships that article is describing, it lands in the balcony: an angled one, where the side of the ship changes width, can be deeper than the balcony on the straight run of the same category. That is a geometry claim, not a promise of a second sofa. If a plan does not print a size, we do not invent one.

Passenger boards discuss the same shape on Celebrity's Solstice-class ships, and they argue about which balcony on the slant is the deep one. Those posts are not a deck plan. This page does not list Celebrity Equinox or Celebrity Solstice as hump ships.

Plans opened on 20 Nov 2026

Carnival's Liberty page has a Download PDF link. The PDF saved that day (two pages, titled Deck Plans, Carnival Liberty) names the decks from Riviera (deck 1) through Sky (deck 14), lists oceanview categories 6A, 6B and 6C, and marks staterooms 2446 and 2449 as obstructed views in category 6B. It prints gross tonnage 110,000 and a double-occupancy capacity of 2,974. It does not use the word hump, and it does not give a square footage for those oceanviews. The side-on drawing shows the usual lifeboat bulge in the profile. A lifeboat bulge is not a hump cabin. Our Liberty cabin guide uses that plan for noise, not for a hump call.

Celebrity's Equinox deck-plan page, saved the same day, does not contain the word hump. We did not find a downloadable plan on that page that labels one.

How to spot one on a plan you have open

Look at the outline of the deck, not the marketing name. A hump is a step or a curve in the side of the ship with cabins along it. Then read the category. If the plan says obstructed, it is an obstructed cabin, which cabins to avoid already covers, whether or not the hull steps out beside it. If the plan gives two sizes for the same category, the larger one is the fact. If it gives one size, or none, the room is not "bigger" in any way we can cite.

Ships we have not opened a plan for are not on this page. Adding a name because a forum uses it would be a guess. The how to choose guide is the decision order when the plan does not show a hump at all: deck, then the spot on the deck, then the room type.

Frequently asked questions

Are hump cabins bigger?

Cruise Critic's guide, checked 20 Nov 2026, says angled balconies where the hull changes width may be roomier than a typical balcony. It does not say the interior is larger. The Carnival Liberty deck plan PDF opened the same day prints no square footage for its oceanviews.

Does Carnival Liberty have hump cabins?

Its official deck plan PDF, downloaded 20 Nov 2026, does not use the word. It does label oceanview staterooms 2446 and 2449 as obstructed views. Those are obstructed cabins, not a cited hump.

Which ships have hump cabins?

None of the official plans opened for this page on 20 Nov 2026 label one. Celebrity Equinox is discussed on passenger boards. Its deck-plan page that day did not contain the word, so it is not listed here.
