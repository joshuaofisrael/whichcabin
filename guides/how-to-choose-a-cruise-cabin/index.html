How to choose a cruise cabin

Short answer: choose the deck first, then the position on the deck, then the room type. A cabin on a deck with cabins above and below, midship and away from elevators, beats a fancier room under the pool deck.

1. Choose the deck

Look at what is directly above and below the deck. The best cabin decks are sandwiched between other cabin decks. The worst for light sleepers sit directly under a pool, sports or buffet deck (deck chairs and set-up start early) or directly above a theatre, casino, nightclub or promenade (busy late). Our quietest decks table lists the sandwiched decks for every ship we cover.

2. Choose the spot on the deck

  • Midship moves least in a swell and is close to the main elevators.
  • Forward cabins feel the most pitching; the CDC advises avoiding the bow if you are prone to motion sickness. The lowest forward cabins are also close to the bow thrusters and anchor gear.
  • Aft cabins can have wide wake views from corner balconies, and are often quiet at night, but some ships have more vibration at the stern.
  • Avoid cabins beside elevator lobbies, stair landings, laundry rooms and service doors (unlabelled blocks on the deck plan) if you are a light sleeper.

3. Choose the room type

  • Interior: cheapest and darkest, good for sleep. Some ships sell virtual-window or promenade-view interiors.
  • Oceanview: a window, which helps with seasickness because you can see the horizon.
  • Balcony: private outdoor space. Read the category: inward-facing, promenade-view and partial-view balconies cost less but give a different view.
  • Suites and ship-within-a-ship areas (The Haven, MSC Yacht Club, The Retreat, Disney Concierge) add space and private areas.

4. Check the category name for warnings

Lines flag compromises in the category name or on the deck plan legend: "partial view", "obstructed view", "inward facing", "promenade view". Cabins one deck above the lifeboats are the usual obstructed-view rooms.

5. Guarantee cabins

A guarantee fare means the line picks your cabin within a category. It is cheaper, but you cannot choose the deck, so it is a poor bet for light sleepers or anyone prone to seasickness.

Worked example: Symphony of the Seas

On Symphony of the Seas, decks 10 to 11 are the sandwiched cabin decks, while deck 14, 17 sits directly under the pool and sports deck. So a midship balcony on deck 11 is a strong all-round pick.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best location for a cruise cabin?

Midship, on a deck with cabin decks directly above and below, and away from elevators and service doors. That combination gives the least motion and the least noise.

Is it better to be on a high or low deck on a cruise ship?

Lower decks move less in rough seas, which helps with seasickness. Higher decks are closer to the pools and buffet and often have more balcony cabins. The deck directly under the pool deck is the one to avoid if you sleep lightly.

Are aft cabins good on a cruise?

Aft cabins can have large wake-view balconies and are often quieter at night. Some ships have more vibration and motion at the stern than midship, so if you are prone to seasickness choose midship instead.

Should I book a guarantee cabin?

Only if price matters more than location. With a guarantee fare the line assigns the cabin, so you cannot avoid noisy or forward decks.
