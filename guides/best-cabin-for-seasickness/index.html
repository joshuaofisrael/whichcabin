Best cruise cabin location for seasickness

Short answer: book a cabin midship on one of the lowest passenger decks, with a window or balcony if you can. That is where the ship moves least, and a view of the horizon reduces symptoms.

Why low and midship

A ship pitches around its middle, so the ends move most and the middle least. The CDC's guidance on motion sickness lists avoiding the locations of maximum motion, such as the bow of a ship, and getting a visual horizon reference among the behavioural countermeasures. Higher decks also sway more in a roll than lower ones.

Window or no window?

The CDC lists obtaining a visual horizon reference as one of the measures shown to give some protection on ships. An oceanview or balcony cabin lets you see the horizon; an interior cabin does not, though you can head to a low, midship public deck with windows instead.

Other things that help

Medication and behavioural tips are covered in the CDC's guidance. This page is general information, not medical advice: talk to a doctor or pharmacist about medication before you sail.

Frequently asked questions

Where should I book a cabin if I get seasick?

Midship on the lowest passenger decks, ideally with a window or balcony so you can see the horizon.

Is an aft cabin bad for seasickness?

Aft is better than forward but usually worse than midship, because the ends of the ship move more when it pitches.

Do balcony cabins help with seasickness?

They can. The CDC lists a visual horizon reference among the countermeasures shown to give some protection on ships, and a balcony or window gives you one.
